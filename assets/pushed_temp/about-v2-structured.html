Mission & Vision

What Pure is,
and what it stands for

Mission

To sell Hue traditional handicrafts and direct half of every sale's net profit to support disadvantaged people in Hue — with a focus on children's health, living conditions, and education.

Vision

A Hue where traditional craft and community support grow together — where buying something beautiful is also an act of lasting, dignified change.

Values

Dignity. Transparency. Long-term thinking. Community-first. Never asking for more than what a fair sale provides.

Our Story

From a decade of personal work
to a formal initiative

For over ten years, the people behind Pure have been doing community work in Hue without an organisation name, without a formal budget, and without asking anything in return. They visited families. They connected people to resources. They showed up during the floods.

This year, they decided to make it official — not to change what they do, but to build something that could grow beyond their personal capacity. Pure Community Support is now legally registered in Vietnam as a commercial website, with two physical offices: one in Hue and one in Da Nang.

It is not a charity. It does not accept donations. It earns its community funding entirely through the sale of traditional Hue handicrafts — objects made by local artisans with real skills and real stories, sold to people who want to bring something of Hue into their lives.

Geographic Focus

Why Hue

Hue is one of Vietnam's most historically significant cities — the former imperial capital of the Nguyen dynasty, home to a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and a living center of Vietnamese traditional craft. Its culinary traditions, architectural heritage, and artisan skills are unlike anywhere else in the country.

It is also a city that bears the weight of Vietnam's most severe seasonal hardship. Central Vietnam — and Hue specifically — faces intense annual typhoon seasons and flooding that regularly disrupts the lives of families who are already navigating difficult circumstances.

Pure focuses on Hue because its founders are rooted here — and because a decade of community work has given them a network, a knowledge of families, and a relationship with local authorities and institutions that cannot be replicated from a distance.

As the initiative grows, geographic reach may expand. For now, the focus is here: Hue and the surrounding area, done well, with care.

How it works

The model

Pure earns its community funding through product sales. Every handcrafted object sold generates a clear, auditable contribution to the support work.

Item Detail
Revenue source Sale of Hue traditional handicrafts — gongs, bells, silk apparel, woven bags, home textiles. All handmade in Hue by local artisans.
Profit split 50% of net profit (after operating costs) goes to community support. The remaining 50% goes back into product development.
Donations Not accepted. When people offer to donate, it is always declined. Revenue comes only from product sales — this is non-negotiable.
Financial oversight One general bank account, opened exclusively for this purpose. All disbursements are tracked and published monthly or quarterly in the blog.
Beneficiary follow-up Every family Pure supports receives a quarterly follow-up visit. A formal annual impact report is planned.
Consent Explicit written consent is required before any beneficiary case or photo appears on this site. Cases without consent are never published.
Areas of focus

Four pillars of action

A decade of community experience in Hue has shaped four clear areas of focus. These pillars reflect both the needs Pure has encountered and the types of support it is best positioned to provide.

1
Community Support
Direct assistance to disadvantaged families — food, essentials, health support. The founding thread of this work, ongoing since well before Pure became official.
2
Culture & Heritage
Preserving Hue's traditional craft skills — gong forging, silk embroidery, hand-weaving — and the cultural context that gives those skills meaning.
3
Education & Youth
Connecting young people in Hue to leadership opportunities and craft traditions through structured programs and mentorship by community practitioners.
4
Crisis Relief
Rapid response for families affected by typhoons, floods, and other emergencies — a recurring need in central Vietnam that the team has responded to for over a decade.
Active programs

Community work in practice

Pure runs four programs continuously. There are no funding targets and no end dates. Three have been operating since before Pure became an official initiative.

Running

Food & Essentials Kit

Regular delivery of food and essential goods to disadvantaged families and individuals in Hue who need ongoing practical support.

Running

Lotus Art for Healing Workshop

Art therapy and creative workshops for people in vulnerable situations — using traditional craft as a medium for wellbeing and connection.

Running

Youth Leadership Circle

A structured program for young people in Hue focused on community engagement, civic responsibility, and building skills that go beyond the classroom.

Planned

Heritage Craft Class

Hands-on instruction in traditional Hue craft techniques, connecting young people directly with artisan practitioners and the cultural knowledge they carry.

How we operate

Our commitments

No donations, ever. When people offer to donate, it is always declined. Revenue comes only from product sales.

Consent before publication. Every beneficiary gives explicit written consent before any case or photograph is published on this site.

Quarterly follow-ups. Every family Pure supports receives a follow-up visit three months after support is given — not to document, but to understand.

Public financial transparency. Monthly and quarterly blog posts show how much was earned and exactly where the money went.

Continuous programs, no goal pressure. Campaigns run indefinitely with no funding targets. Support is not conditional on hitting a number.

Founders stay behind the scenes. This initiative is about community and craft. No founder names or photos are prominently featured on this site.

The makers

Hue artisans and collaborators

Every product in the Pure catalog is the work of a specific person — a Hue artisan practicing a skill with a history that long predates this organisation. Their names, stories, and craft traditions are documented in the product catalog and the blog.

A dedicated artisan recognition section is maintained on this site, listing every artisan and collaborator who has contributed to Pure's products — including those who designed, created, or helped develop what you see in the catalog. Beyond the makers, a broader group of volunteers and collaborators makes this work possible. If you are interested in contributing, get in touch.
