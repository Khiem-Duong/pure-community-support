About Pure

A Decade of Quiet Work.
Now, Something More.

Pure Community Support is a registered community initiative in Hue, Vietnam. We sell traditional Hue handicrafts made by local artisans. Half of every sale's net profit goes directly to support disadvantaged families in Hue — with a focus on children's health, living conditions, and education.

The Imperial City, Hue

Our story

From personal work
to a formal initiative

For over a decade, the founders of Pure and a close circle of friends have been showing up for their community — quietly, without an organisation name, without a budget, and without asking for anything in return. They visited families, connected people to resources, and funded small acts of support from their own pockets.

This year, they decided to make it official. Not to change what they do, but to open it up — so that more people could participate, more resources could flow in, and the work could continue on a foundation that doesn't depend on any one person's capacity.

Pure Community Support is legally registered in Vietnam as a commercial website with two physical offices: one in Hue and one in Da Nang. It is not a charity. It does not accept public donations. It earns its funding through the sale of handcrafted goods made by Hue artisans.

The work doesn't start when someone donates. It starts when someone chooses to buy something made in Hue — and understands what that choice means.

Why Hue

A city of culture,
resilience, and need

Hue sits at the heart of Vietnam's history. Once the imperial capital of the Nguyen dynasty, it remains one of the country's most culturally rich cities — home to a UNESCO World Heritage Site, a distinct culinary tradition, and craft techniques passed down through generations.

It is also a city that faces some of Vietnam's most severe seasonal hardships. Central Vietnam bears the brunt of annual typhoon seasons, and Hue has repeatedly experienced flooding that disrupts lives, damages homes, and pushes already vulnerable families into crisis. For the families Pure works with, these aren't abstract risks — they are recurring realities.

Pure is rooted in Hue because the founders are rooted in Hue. The geographic focus — Hue and the surrounding area — reflects both the network the team has built over a decade and a commitment to knowing the communities they serve. As the initiative grows, that reach may expand.

The model

Commerce as a means,
not an end

Pure does not ask for money. It earns it. Every product sold is a handcrafted object made in Hue by local artisans. Every purchase is a direct act of support — not through charity, but through commerce that is built to give back.

Ceremonial gong & mallet, handcrafted in Hue

01

You buy a Hue handicraft

Every product is handmade in Hue by local artisans. Traditional objects, silk apparel, woven bags, and home textiles — made with care.

02

50% of net profit is set aside

After operating costs, exactly half of net profit is allocated to community support. The other half goes back into product development.

03

Funds reach families in Hue

Support goes to disadvantaged families — primarily focused on children's health, education, and living conditions, plus elderly, disabled, and crisis victims.

Funds are held in a single bank account opened exclusively for this purpose. All disbursements are tracked and published monthly or quarterly in the blog. A formal annual impact report is planned. Every family Pure supports has given explicit consent to be featured.

Areas of focus

Four pillars of action

Pure's community work is organised around four areas, reflecting a decade of hands-on experience with what Hue families actually need.

Community Support

Direct assistance to disadvantaged families in Hue — food and essentials kits, health support, and crisis relief. The original, ongoing thread of work that predates the organisation itself.

Culture & Heritage

Preserving and promoting Hue's distinctive craft traditions — from the ceremonial gong and singing bowl to embroidery and weaving techniques passed across generations.

Education & Youth

Supporting young people in Hue through the Youth Leadership Circle and Heritage Craft Class — connecting the next generation to the skills and community roots that define their city.

Crisis Relief

Rapid response support for families affected by typhoons, floods, and other emergencies — a recurring need in central Vietnam that the team has responded to for over a decade.

Our commitments

Pure is built on a set of firm operating principles — not policies written for show, but decisions made over years of experience with what works and what doesn't.

We never accept donations. When people offer money, we always decline. Revenue comes only from product sales.

Consent is non-negotiable. Every beneficiary gives explicit written consent before any case or photo appears on this site.

We follow up. Every family Pure supports receives a quarterly follow-up visit to understand how their situation has evolved.

We publish our numbers. Monthly and quarterly blog posts show how much was earned and exactly where the money went.

Programs run without goals. Our campaigns are continuous — not fundraisers with a target date. Support doesn't stop when a number is hit.

The founders stay behind the scenes. This initiative is about community and craft, not personal profiles. No founder names or photos are prominently featured.

The makers

Hue artisans and collaborators

Every product in the Pure catalog is the work of a person — a Hue artisan with a specific skill, a specific history, and a specific reason to still be practicing their craft. Pure exists partly because of them, and they are recognised in full in the product catalog, the blog, and in a dedicated section of this site.

Traditional craft, living skill

The makers behind Pure's products practice craft traditions that define Hue — from gong and bell forging to silk embroidery and hand-weaving. Their work is not a revival project. These are active skills, passed down through families and communities who have kept them alive through disruption, war, and economic change.

Collaborators and supporters

Beyond the artisans, a broader group of volunteers and collaborators has made Pure possible — people who source, coordinate, photograph, translate, and connect. They are not named here, but they are part of what this project is. If you are interested in contributing, get in touch.
